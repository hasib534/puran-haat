# Haat Cycle — icon design philosophy

**Movement: Haat Cycle.**

An icon should feel like a found object from the place it names: worn smooth by handling, warm to the touch, and unmistakably *of* a market rather than a software category. Haat Cycle treats the app mark as a carved seal — a single closed form pressed into deep forest ground, made with the patience of someone who has cut the same shape a thousand times and still adjusts the last millimetre.

The composition is one continuous gesture: two rounded arrow bands chase each other around a shared centre, each head resting where the other began. Between them, one leaf grows upward from the empty space, its weight pitched low and its stem the smallest possible interruption. Nothing is decorative. Every edge exists because the form demanded it, and the eye should never find a seam, a wobble, or a corner that was not deliberate.

Colour is reduced to three voices and none of them compete. Deep forest green is the ground — not a background but soil. Unbleached cream carries the cycle, because cream reads at any size and behaves like ink on paper. A single warm clay leaf supplies the only warmth, the way a terracotta pot is the only warm thing in a courtyard. Where light is available, a whisper of vertical gradient keeps the field from going flat without ever becoming a texture.

Scale is the discipline. The mark must hold at 1024 pixels and at 32, so the leaf is drawn as a silhouette first and a leaf second, and the arrows carry no interior detail that would dissolve. The negative space between the cycle and the leaf is treated as an active material — wide enough to breathe, tight enough that the three elements read as one object rather than a diagram. Symmetry is the armature; the leaf's taper, its low-slung belly, and the slight curve of its vein are the hand that breaks the machine.

Execution is everything. Curves are built from arcs and matched control points, never eyeballed. Arrow tails are true radii, heads are radial wedges tangent to the ring, and the leaf's stem is the same width as the vein's breath so the eye follows one line from the ring to the tip. The result should look like it took weeks — like a stamp cut by a master engraver, then printed once, perfectly, on the first try.

The mark is deliberately quiet about its meaning. Those who know the app see reuse, exchange, and the old market at once; everyone else sees a confident circular emblem with a leaf at its heart, and trusts it. That is the whole ambition: meaning held in proportion, not explained.
